Whitespace is often treated as decoration, but it is first of all a structural tool. The distance between paragraphs, between a heading and its body text, and between list items determines whether a reader can see the hierarchy within seconds.

## Set the rhythm first, then tune the details

I start by splitting the page into a few stable rhythms:

- tight spacing between a heading and its summary;
- a clear but not excessive gap between one item and the next;
- the same spacing set for elements in the same group, with no ad-hoc nudging.

Once the rhythm is stable, the exact pixels stop mattering.

## Dividers are not the only tool

Dividers between list items reinforce the sense that these are separate entries, but they also add visual noise. If the entries already have a clear title and date hierarchy, removing the dividers and relying on spacing alone is often quieter.

## Whitespace should serve reading

Decorative whitespace and structural whitespace are not the same thing. The former makes a page look polished; the latter helps a reader find the next paragraph faster. Design should prioritize the latter.
